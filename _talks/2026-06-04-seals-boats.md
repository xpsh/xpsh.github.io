Second author, presented together with Alexander D. Smith, Jian Yu, and Peter Schuelke 
Abstract: Austronesian migration into Oceania is one of the great feats of humanity and involved important  material innovations in watercraft technology. The presence of some watercraft innovations at the  Proto-Austronesian and Proto-Malayo-Polynesian level are informed by linguistic reconstruction. If  terms for specific watercraft innovations are reconstructable to a particular level, then we can assume  that the referenced technology was indeed present at that level. In this study, we reanalyze the  linguistic evidence for watercraft and seafaring terminology and find that much of the reconstructed  vocabulary suffers from irregularities that cast doubt on their reconstructability. Rather, we find that  key innovations likely spread after PMP migration out of Taiwan. 
We find that of the four PAN watercraft lexical reconstructions, *qabaŋ ‘boat’, *aluja ‘to paddle’,  *layaR ‘sail’, and *nimas ‘to bail water’, only *aluja is well supported at the PAN level. At the PMP  level, *layaR ‘sail’, *aluja ‘boat’, and *limas ‘to bail water’ remain well supported, as do some PMP  innovations, including *bəRsay ‘paddle’, but others, including the important PMP reconstruction  *saRman ‘outrigger’, are not reconstructable to PMP unless one assumes massive phonological  irregularity. This suggests that the innovation of the outrigger was not the event that triggered  Austronesian migration from Taiwan. 
The lack of linguistic evidence is complemented by theories on outrigger evolution, which claim that  the double-hulled canoe was innovated first, with outriggers developing more recently (Mahdi 1999).  Additionally, the phonological irregularity outrigger words typically arise through contact, not  inheritance (Grimes & Edwards to appear, Smith 2023). Furthermore, we run oceanic voyaging  simulations which demonstrate the centrality of the sail as necessary for voyaging to the Philippines.  All of this suggests that the assignment of certain innovations to the PMP speaking community and  the assumption that those innovations were precursors for the migration from Taiwan to the  Philippines, are unwarranted. Rather, the evolution of watercraft technology occurred over time in a  widely dispersed post-expansion Austronesian community.
